We have used matplotlib to generate the following plots, which are rolling parameter 
estimates of the mean and vol terms of both stocks for {2, 5, 10} year windows, generated 
using PX_LAST. 
For the given two stocks, the parameters of mean and vol, are estimated using a rolling 
window of 2, 5, 10 years, of trading days, which we assume to be 252. The assumption that 
mu and vol are constant as stated in the Q, means that within the window of [t – l, t], we 
treated the GBM drift and vol terms as a constant, and “fit” the two values. Unweighted 
fitting, means that every daily return, received equal importance, which is not as methodically 
sound, as previous value importance decays. We computed daily log returns from PX_last, 
and we sample the mean and stdev and annualised using the 252-trading day conventions.  
Notably, the computed parameters are comparably, because we are purely estimating the 
return parameters from the log returns, which are scale free. It doesn’t not matter for the price 
level or dollars invested. So even if AAPL and CAT prices, and by extension, the dollar value 
of a certain position can change immensely across 20 years, the estimated drift and volatility 
are still comparable across the time, because it articulates the distribution of % changes, not 
the distribution of dollar terms.  
On an overall basis, the volatility term looks reasonably stable in the long-term but is clearly 
dependent on regimes. Drift is much less stable. The general trend, as expected is that the 
shorter windows, especially 2 years, are far less stable, whereas 10 years is the most stable. 
 


 
For instance, looking at the volatility terms across the two equities, we can see that the 2Y 
window is extremely jumpy at stress periods, like during the GFC, and during the beginning 
stages of COVID, before quickly mean-reverting. 5Y tends to be a bit smoother. 10Y 
window, in this case, does seem to move slowly, and does capture long-run volatility. 
Pronounced spikes around major stress events, as mentioned prior, and followed by 
normalisation, does indicate generic volatility clustering. 
 
 
 
 
 


 
 
On the other hand, the drift term is extremely noisy, and fluctuates widely, indicating that 
overall, the short-horizon drift estimation is not reliable. The 5Y drift is also quite variable, 
but the 10Y drift is the only one that looks somewhat stable, and differs across the different 
market regimes, like the recovery from 2008, but is probably extremely noisy, and has many 
confoundment factors, since it also covers the dotcom bubble burst. 
Interestingly enough, volatility is stable enough for some interpretation, especially 5Y/10Y , 
but clearly affected by regimes. Drift is extremely noisy at the 2y marks, so for unweighted 
windows, it is worth comparing different l sizes.

In the Q3 with unweighted fitting, each date t and window length l, GBM was estimated 
using the last l  years, with each observation equal weight, but in Q4, the same rolling 
estimation is completed.  
Notably, with the trailing window ending at weight are assigned such that decay is 
geometrically backward in time, such that the most recent returns has the largest weight, and 
a return k days old, has the weight proportional to (1 − λ)λ𝑘. Notably, a smaller λ decays 


faster, so it places more emphasis on the last few weeks, and as such, it means that it is 
noisier. Vice versa for larger λ. 
As volatility is driven by squared returns, if the market has a recent shock, exponential 
weighing makes those recent weightings dominate the returns. This means there are faster vol 
spikes in periods of stresses, and faster rise & decay. We expect this to be the case, for λ =  
0.94 to swing more sharply than 0.97.  
 
In addressing the parts, the estimated parameters are comparable over time because they are 
computed from log-returns, which are scale-free % returns. Drift and vol describe the 
distribution of proportional returns, not dollar levels. So, even if the AAPL and CAT prices 
differ substantively in the time, the estimated parameters remain directly comparable. 
Exponential doesn’t change anything. In terms of stability, exponential weighting is 
materially changed, because recent observations, receive larger weights, vol responds more 
quickly to shocks and also decays more rapidly once the shock passes, with sharper spikes. 
However, this responsiveness, is at the cost of higher variability, with vol becoming more 
reactive, with drift becoming even noisier under exponential weighting, as such there is a 
trade-off.
